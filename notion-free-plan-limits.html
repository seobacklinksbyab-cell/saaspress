Notion Free Plan Limits: What Actually Caps You (2026)

By AdminNovember 4, 2026Reading Time: 4 minutes

Notion's free plan is often described as unlimited, and for one person it nearly is. The trouble starts when you share files, invite clients or try to restore an old version of a page. This guide explains the Notion free plan limits that actually matter, which old limits no longer apply, and when a freelancer should think about paying.

Short answer: if you work alone, the Notion free plan is enough for most freelance work. The limits you will notice first are the 5 MB cap on each uploaded file, the 10 guest limit and the 7 days of page history.

Notion free plan limits at a glance

As of 4 November 2026, these are the limits people most often run into:

  • Pages and blocks: unlimited when you are the only member of the workspace.
  • File uploads: up to 5 MB per file.
  • Guests: up to 10 external guests.
  • Page history: 7 days.
  • Notion AI: a limited trial, not a full free feature.
  • Team workspaces: if two or more people are members, a cap of 1,000 blocks applies to the workspace.

The 1,000-block limit: who does it affect?

Many older articles still say that Notion's free plan stops at 1,000 blocks. That is only true for free workspaces with two or more members. A block is any single piece of content, such as a paragraph, a to-do item, a database row or an image. If you are the only member, you can add as many blocks and pages as you like.

For a freelancer, the practical rule is simple: keep your own workspace for yourself and share work with clients as guests, not as members.

The 5 MB file limit

This is the limit most freelancers hit first. A screenshot, a PDF proposal or a design export can easily go over 5 MB, and Notion will refuse the upload. You have three easy ways around it:

  • Store large files in Google Drive or Dropbox and paste the link into your Notion page.
  • Compress images and PDFs before you upload them.
  • Embed a shared file instead of uploading it.

Guests: how clients can see your work

Guests are people you invite to a specific page. On the free plan you can have up to 10, and they only see the pages you share with them. For a freelancer this is useful: you can give each client a page with their tasks, notes and deadlines without adding them to your workspace. Check Notion's help pages for exactly how guests are counted before you promise access to many clients.

Page history is only 7 days

If you delete a paragraph or overwrite a page, you can only restore versions from the past week. If a page is important, such as a contract summary or a client brief, export it as PDF or Markdown from time to time so you have your own copy.

Is the Notion free plan enough for a freelancer?

  • Solo freelancer with a few clients: yes. Use one workspace, one page per client and links for large files.
  • Freelancer who shares files with clients: mostly yes, but the 5 MB limit will push you towards cloud storage links.
  • Two people sharing one workspace: this is where the 1,000-block cap can become a problem, so plan for a paid plan or keep work in separate personal workspaces.

When it makes sense to upgrade

Think about the Plus plan if you regularly upload files bigger than 5 MB, need more than 10 guests, want a longer page history or share a workspace with another member. At the time of writing, Plus starts at about $10 per member per month when billed annually. If none of those problems affects you, there is no reason to pay.

Our verdict

Notion's free plan is generous for solo work, and the famous block limit is not a problem unless you share the workspace with other members. Plan around the file size and page history, and you can stay on the free plan for a long time.
