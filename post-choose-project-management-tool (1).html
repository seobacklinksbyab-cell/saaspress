How to choose a project management tool: 7 questions to ask first

SaaS comparisons · Updated October 16, 2026 · 6 min read

Most people pick project management software the same way: they search "best project management tools", try the most popular name, and switch months later when it does not fit. The fix is simple. Start with your work, not with the tool.

These seven questions take about ten minutes. Write down your answers before you open a single pricing page.

1. Who will use it, and who only needs to look?

Count everyone who will add or change tasks. Then count people who only need to see progress, such as clients or a manager. Many tools treat these two groups differently, and the way a plan counts them can decide whether a free plan works for you.

2. What does your work look like?

Pick the closest description:

  • Stages. Work moves from "to do" to "doing" to "done". A board with cards suits this.
  • A list with due dates. You mostly need to know what is due and who owns it. A task list is enough.
  • A schedule with dependencies. One task cannot start until another finishes. You need a timeline view.

3. Do you plan by date?

If deadlines drive your week, check for a calendar view. If you never think in dates, you can skip it and keep the tool simpler.

4. Do clients need to see progress?

Freelancers and agencies should check how a tool shares work with people outside the team. Look for guest access or a shareable view, and confirm what a guest can and cannot change.

5. How much setup will you tolerate?

Some tools give you a blank canvas and let you build your own system. Notion works this way, with pages and databases you design yourself. That is powerful, but you build the structure. Board-style tools such as Trello are quicker to start because the structure is already there.

6. What does the free plan actually limit?

Free plans usually limit some mix of users, views, automations, storage and history. They also change. Reports on the free Personal plan of Asana, for example, give different user limits depending on when the account was created, so confirm the current limit before you commit.

How to check a free plan in 3 minutes: open the vendor's own pricing page, find the plan comparison table, and write down the limits that touch your answers to questions 1 to 4. Note the date you checked.

7. How easy is it to leave?

Before you add real work, look for an export option, such as CSV. If you cannot get your data out, switching later gets harder every month.

A simple way to decide

  1. Shortlist two tools that match your answers.
  2. Move one real project into each for one week.
  3. Keep the one you opened without being reminded.
