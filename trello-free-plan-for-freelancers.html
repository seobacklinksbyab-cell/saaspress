Trello Free Plan for Freelancers: What You Really Get

By AdminNovember 3, 2026Reading Time: 4 minutes

Trello is one of the first tools freelancers try, mostly because the free plan looks generous. It is, but only up to a point. This guide explains what the Trello free plan includes, where it stops, and how to tell whether it is enough for your freelance work.

Short answer: for a solo freelancer with a handful of clients, the free plan is usually enough. The limits that matter are the number of boards, the number of people you can add, and the size of attached files.

What you get on the Trello free plan

As of 3 November 2026, the free plan includes the following:

  • Up to 10 boards per Workspace. This is the limit most freelancers hit first.
  • Up to 10 collaborators per Workspace.
  • Unlimited cards and lists. You can add as many tasks as you need on each board.
  • Unlimited Power-Ups per board. Power-Ups connect Trello to other tools such as calendars and file storage.
  • 250 Workspace command runs per month. These power Trello's built-in automation, such as moving a card when a due date passes.
  • 10 MB per file attachment.
  • An inbox for quick notes, mobile apps and two-factor authentication.

What the free plan does not include

Trello keeps several features for paid plans. On the free plan you will not get unlimited boards or custom fields, and the extra views such as Calendar, Timeline, Table and Dashboard are part of the higher paid plans. In practice this means the free plan is a simple board of lists and cards, which suits most one-person workflows well.

Is the free plan enough for a freelancer?

It depends on how you organise your work. Here are three common setups:

  • One board per client. This works well until you have around ten active boards. Keep one extra board for your own admin, such as invoices and marketing.
  • One board for everything. Use one list per client or per stage (Leads, In progress, Waiting for feedback, Done). This never runs into the board limit.
  • Clients who need access. If you invite clients to your boards, remember the 10 collaborator limit per Workspace. Check Trello's help pages to see exactly how guests are counted before you rely on it.

The 10 MB file limit

Design files, long videos and large PDFs often go over 10 MB. Instead of attaching them, paste a link to the file in your cloud storage. A link attached to a card works the same way and does not use any file limit.

When it makes sense to upgrade

Think about a paid plan when you regularly need more than 10 boards, want custom fields to track things like budget or deadline per project, or use a lot of automation. Trello's Standard plan is the first paid step, and at the time of writing it starts at $5 per user per month when billed annually. For one freelancer, that is only worth paying for if the board limit or custom fields are slowing you down.

Our verdict

Trello's free plan is a good fit if you want a simple visual board, you have fewer than ten active projects, and you do not need to share files larger than 10 MB. If you outgrow it, you do not have to pay straight away: a single board with a list per client keeps you inside the free plan for a long time.
